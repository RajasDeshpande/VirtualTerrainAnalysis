What your terrain actually tells you

This project turns measured elevation data into a 3D surface. It also calculates slope, measures height differences and attaches real imagery. Each of these layers answers a different question.

View or valueWhat it means
ImageryThe photographed appearance of the area when the source imagery was acquired. Roads, vegetation and buildings help you recognize the place. The picture is draped on the elevation mesh.
ElevationA point's vertical height relative to the dataset's datum. Copernicus uses the EGM2008 geoid. The yellow end of the elevation palette marks the highest point in this selected area.
SlopeThe angle of a triangle to the horizontal: blue up to 10°, green up to 25°, yellow up to 35°, orange up to 45°, red above 45°. A high flat plateau can be blue.
Drop or riseSelect A and B. B minus A gives the signed height difference. A valley's depth depends on which rim or reference point you choose.
DistanceHorizontal separation and the direct 3D line between A and B. Neither is a hiking route or distance along the terrain surface.

Resolution and accuracy are different

A 30-metre elevation grid has samples about 30 metres apart. A road or ditch only a few metres wide may be absent. Interpolation can make the display smooth, but it cannot create missing measurements.

A sharp image may show a roof clearly while the elevation underneath still comes from 30-metre samples. Changing to imagery mode improves recognition, not elevation accuracy. This viewer applies no vertical exaggeration.

Use the built-in global model for regional landforms and exploratory analysis. For site operations, engineering, drainage details or precise measurements, use an appropriate survey/LiDAR DEM and compare against known ground control. The app does not certify local accuracy or traversability.

Copernicus GLO-30 is a surface model, so vegetation and structures may affect heights. Its published vertical-accuracy specification is under 4 metres at 90% confidence; that is a dataset specification, not a measured error for your selected patch. The AWS release used here is 2021; primary acquisitions are from 2011–2015 with possible older infill. Recent earthworks may be missing.

What happens when you press Build

  1. Your chosen coordinates and area define a square in a local metric coordinate system.
  2. The data-preparation step reads only the needed elevation window and projects it into metres. It records the source, date information, grid spacing and height datum. Missing coverage stops the build.
  3. BlenderGIS imports that GeoTIFF and creates the terrain mesh from its samples.
  4. The analysis script triangulates the surface, calculates slopes from the geometry, assigns export-safe colors and writes elevation/slope statistics and CSV files.
  5. The checks compare exported heights to the source raster, check every slope classification, and verify that imagery coordinates align with the real map extent.
  6. Only a successful build becomes the current browser terrain. Earlier builds remain available in the project folders.

Using your own detailed survey

Choose My survey / LiDAR GeoTIFF on the chooser page. Enter the path to a georeferenced, single-band elevation TIFF on this PC, confirm metre elevation units, and describe its vertical datum. Select an area fully covered by that file. The mesh is capped at about 500 cells per side for practical VR use; the viewer reports the resulting grid spacing if a larger survey area needs a coarser grid.

Downloads include the projected DEM, elevation sample CSV, triangle slope CSV, statistics JSON and verification report. The check report demonstrates that the model matches its input; ground truth requires an independent survey.

Useful next improvements

For operations near you, first obtain a recent high-resolution bare-earth DEM and survey control points. Then add the operation-specific data you need: drainage and flood levels, land cover and obstacles, or road/trail condition. A slope-only map cannot determine whether a surface is passable.
